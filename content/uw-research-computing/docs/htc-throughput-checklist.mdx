Whether you're submitting 1 job or 10,000 jobs,
this checklist has easy changes that can help you get better throughput on the HTC system.

* Reduce your resource requests
* Remove unnecessary requirements
* Scale out to additional capacity

Take a few minutes to check your submit file to see if one these tips could work for you!

## Reduce your resource requests

Are you requesting more resources than your job is actually using?
Those "wasted" resources are dragging down your throughput!

1. Check your actual usage
2. Reduce your request
3. Automatically retry rare overusage

<Tip header="Testing is okay">

When you are testing while developing a workflow,
don't worry about the resource usage.

Once your test job works as intended,
then start tuning the resource requests.

</Tip>

but can be difficult to utilize when you're running thousands of jobs. 

#### Use job history

<!-- Needs cleaning up... -->
If the jobs you are interested in have finished running within the last couple of days or so,
then HTCondor should have a record of them in the AP's history.

You can query this information with a command like this:

<Terminal>

```bash
condor_history CLUSTER_ID -af:hj, CpusUsage RequestCPUs MemoryUsage RequestMemory DiskUsage RequestDisk
```

</Terminal>

Example:

```
[aowen4@ap2001 testing]$ condor_history 12367708 -af:hh, CpusUsage RequestCPUs MemoryUsage RequestMemory DiskUsage RequestDisk
CpusUsage, RequestCPUs, MemoryUsage, RequestMemory, DiskUsage, RequestDisk
0.0      , 4          , 0          , 16384        , 75       , 31457280
[aowen4@ap2001 testing]$ condor_history 12367708 -af:hj, CpusUsage RequestCPUs MemoryUsage RequestMemory DiskUsage RequestDisk
 ID, CpusUsage, RequestCPUs, MemoryUsage, RequestMemory, DiskUsage, RequestDisk
12367708.0, 0.0      , 4          , 0          , 16384        , 75       , 31457280
```

### Reduce your request

The goal is to make sure your jobs have *just enough* resources to execute their work.
 The simplest approach is to take your usage values and round up your request to the nearest whole number.

For example, if your job(s)

* used 1.3 CPUs, then set `request_cpus = 2`.
* used 3.5 GB RAM, then set `request_memory = 4GB`.
* used 15.8 GB of disk, then set `request_disk = 16GB`.

In general, we recommend that you request at least 1 CPU, 1GB of memory, and 1GB of disk.
You can technically go lower in terms of memory and disk, but it may not actually affect the amount that your job receives.

### Automatically retry rare overusage

Do most of your jobs work just fine, but a couple will run out of resources?
Most users decide to increase their resource requests to handle these rare cases.
But with an HTCondor feature you can request the low tier amount
and automatically retry jobs that fail with the high tier amount.

<Terminal>

```
request_memory = 2GB
retry_request_memory = 10GB
```

</Terminal>

## Remove unnecessary requirements

1. Testing-based requirements (is build job, avoid CHTC pool for checkpointing)
2. Hardware-based requirements - do you actually need them?

## Scale out with backfill and the OSPool

Get access to more machines. 

1. Use "is_resumable" backfill to leverage idle CHTC servers
2. Use "want_campus_pools" to run on idle UW-Madison servers connected to CHTC
3. Use "want_ospool" to run on idle servers available through the national OSPool system

<Tip header="❔ Shared or backfill">

If you configure your jobs to use the backfill capacity at CHTC,
then those jobs will not be able to run on the shared capacity.
This is an implementation detail that requires strategic changes to the system to correct. 

To utilize both the shared capacity and the backfill capacity,
we recommend that you divide your work between the two submission types.
For example, submit 1,000 jobs that use the shared capacity and
a different 1,000 jobs that use the backfill capacity.

Your request for campus pools and OSPool capacity is independent of this choice.

</Tip>
